# Show Two Payment Choices, Not Ten

ANGKON’s checkout shows many payment brands because it treats several SSLCOMMERZ channels—bKash, Nagad and cards—as separate choices before sending customers to the same hosted gateway. That is a channel-filtering design, not a set of separate payment integrations. Bangladesh retailer examples more commonly present the simpler top-level choice of **Cash on Delivery (COD) or prepaid online payment**. Given ANGKON’s hosted SSLCOMMERZ integration, the clearest and most accurate checkout is COD plus one “Pay online” option; SSLCOMMERZ can then show the channels actually enabled for the merchant.

## Evidence: channel choices share one gateway

In ANGKON’s checkout component (`src/app/checkout/_components/payment-method-step.tsx`), COD sits beside an online-payment card, while the expanded card displays wallet, card and bank logos. Some channel buttons set bKash, Nagad or card as the selected method; other displayed brands and bank tiles are not selectable. The server then maps bKash, Nagad or cards to `multi_card_name` and passes that value into the SSLCOMMERZ session request (`src/server/payments/services/initiate-payment.ts`, `src/server/payments/providers/sslcommerz/create-session.ts`). The customer is redirected to SSLCOMMERZ’s hosted page. So the many choices reflect both the UI’s channel-by-channel presentation and an optional request to filter the gateway’s channel list—not separate gateways. SSLCOMMERZ documents hosted checkout as displaying payment channels after redirect, and says omitting `multi_card_name` displays all available channels. ([SSLCOMMERZ integration guide](https://sslcommerz.com/integration-document/))

The retailer examples reviewed—Daraz, Aarong and Chaldal—present COD alongside online payment, with online options such as cards and bKash; the exact channels differ. This supports a common UX pattern, **not a quantified claim about national payment shares or customer preference**. ([Daraz payment help](https://helpcenter.daraz.com.bd/page/knowledge?pageId=11&category=1000004617&knowledge=1000080453&language=en); [Aarong billing and payments](https://aarong.com/bgd/billing-and-payments); [Chaldal FAQ](https://chaldal.com/t/Help))

## Evidence: provider-wide coverage is not store availability

SSLCOMMERZ advertises cards, mobile and internet banking, wallets and other channels, but says channel activation depends on merchant/bank acceptance. Its session response identifies active gateways; public provider lists do not establish which methods are enabled for ANGKON. ([SSLCOMMERZ home page](https://sslcommerz.com/); [merchant terms, §2.3](https://signup.sslcommerz.com/term-condition); [integration guide](https://sslcommerz.com/integration-document/)) Therefore the checkout’s brand strip and “all major digital channels are fully supported” copy overstate what the public evidence—and the integration alone—can confirm. The live merchant configuration must be checked before naming specific wallets or banks.

## Recommendation: separate COD from online, not channels

Show two top-level options: **“Cash on delivery”** and **“Pay online via SSLCOMMERZ”**, with a short explanation that cards, mobile banking and internet banking options appear on SSLCOMMERZ’s secure payment page. For this generic online route, omit `multi_card_name` so the hosted page can present its available channels. Do not promise bKash, Nagad, Rocket, Upay, specific card brands, or named banks until each is confirmed for ANGKON’s production account and tested. If ANGKON intentionally wants a customer to preselect a channel, retain only confirmed choices and make clear that selection filters the SSLCOMMERZ page; avoid describing it as a direct or independent wallet/card integration.

This matches familiar local checkout organization while accurately describing the technical handoff. Keep COD’s amount due and any eligibility limits clear, and verify the live gateway’s active-channel response before launch; provider marketing counts are not a merchant-specific availability list.
